Issue 01

Housing

California is short roughly 2.5 million homes. Both candidates agree supply must grow — but they split on dense urban housing vs. suburban starter homes, and on how aggressively to override local control.

Dense & State-DrivenSuburban & Deregulate
XBBecerra
SHHilton
53 pts apart on urban-density vs. suburban focus
Becerra (D)

Declare housing state of emergency on day 1. Unstick 40,000 homes stalled in funding. Build 1.5–2 million homes in first term. Push cities to zone for apartments/duplexes/condos; crack down on cities that block new housing.

Source: The Real Deal, campaign
Hilton (R)

"Bring back the starter home." Cap government fees on new homes at $50K. Block CEQA lawsuits used to stop construction. Prefer suburban over dense city apartments. State loan program for first-time buyers.

Source: The Real Deal, CalMatters
Issue 02

Homelessness

California has roughly 180,000 unhoused people — the largest total in the U.S. The debate: prevention and services, or enforcement and treatment mandates.

Prevent & TreatEnforce & Mandate
XBBecerra
SHHilton
33 pts apart on enforcement vs. prevention
Becerra (D)

Clear encampments and hold local governments accountable, while investing in mental health and substance use treatment. Prevention-first: "cheaper to help Californians stay housed than help them off the street." Won't criminalize sleeping outside.

Source: CalMatters
Hilton (R)

"Status quo is not working." Favors enforcement, reversing decarceration, mandating treatment for addiction and mental illness. Has not released a detailed plan.

Source: CalMatters
Issue 03

Taxes

California has the nation's highest top-marginal income tax (13.3%). Candidates split on preserving the progressive structure or cutting and flattening it.

Progressive & ExpandCut & Flatten
XBBecerra
SHHilton
65 pts apart on tax structure
Becerra (D)

Preserve California's progressive income tax. Supports extending the higher rate on high earners to pay for schools. Use the tax base to defend state services from federal cuts.

Source: CNN debate
Hilton (R)

Eliminate state income tax on the first $150,000 of earnings. Flat rate above that threshold. Promises broader fiscal consolidation.

Source: CNN debate
Issue 04

Climate & Energy

California's 2035 gas-car ban and clean energy mandates are among the most aggressive in the U.S. Preserve, pragmatically adjust, or roll back?

Keep MandatesRoll Back
XBBecerra
SHHilton
58 pts apart on climate policy direction
Becerra (D)

Supports the clean energy transition, but open to revising goals if they make energy unaffordable — including Newsom's 2035 gas-car ban. Pragmatic preservation of the climate framework.

Source: CalMatters
Hilton (R)

Roll back clean energy and GHG rules. Favor consumer-friendly policies. Boost oil drilling. Slash environmental regulations.

Source: CalMatters
Issue 05

Immigration

California's sanctuary law bars most state cooperation with federal immigration enforcement. The Trump administration has challenged it. Candidates differ sharply on whether to defend or dismantle it.

Defend SanctuaryComply with ICE
XBBecerra
SHHilton
80 pts apart — the sharpest contrast of this race
Becerra (D)

Defend California's sanctuary law. Refuse to help ICE with civil immigration enforcement. Fight Trump in court — building on his AG tenure when he sued the first Trump administration ~100 times.

Source: CalMatters
Hilton (R)

Cooperate with federal immigration enforcement. Treat sanctuary law as unconstitutional. Back Trump's approach. Favor deportation enforcement and state-federal alignment.

Source: CNN debate
Issue 06

Crime & Public Safety

Voters passed Prop 36 in 2024 to roll back some soft-on-crime provisions. The debate now: how aggressively to enforce vs. how to prevent and treat the underlying drivers.

Prevent & TreatEnforce & Punish
XBBecerra
SHHilton
40 pts apart on enforcement intensity
Becerra (D)

Voted for Prop 36 and would fully fund its provisions. Ensure defendants get court-mandated drug treatment to avoid jail. Treats crime and addiction as problems to prevent and treat first.

Source: CalMatters
Hilton (R)

Enforce existing laws. Reverse decarceration policies of the past decade. Require treatment for addiction and mental illness. Enforcement as primary deterrent.

Source: CalMatters
Issue 07

Education

California's K-12 scores lag national averages on reading and math. Affordability + public school focus vs. structural reforms including charters and vouchers.

Public School & AffordabilityCharter / Voucher
XBBecerra
SHHilton
64 pts apart on school choice
Becerra (D)

Long pro-public-school, anti-voucher record. Treats education mainly as an affordability issue — expand childcare support and workforce training. No major K-12 policy proposals specific to this race.

Source: EdSource
Hilton (R)

Mandate phonics. Hold back 3rd graders not reading at grade level. Make it easier for districts to fire teachers and for parents to petition to convert schools to charters. Public funds for vouchers.

Source: EdSource
