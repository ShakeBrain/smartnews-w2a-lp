Why California matters this cycle

A new map, a reshuffled slate.

Proposition 50, passed by California voters earlier this year, redrew the state's congressional map for the first time since 2020. Several districts shifted in partisan composition — making some previously safe seats competitive and reshaping how both parties approach the 2026 cycle. Cook Political Report has issued 11 ratings changes for California alone, more than any other state. The results could help determine which party holds the House in 2027.

CA-13
Lean Dem
Central Valley · Stockton, Modesto, Merced area

The 2024 race here was decided by 187 votes — the narrowest House margin in the country. Democrat Adam Gray, the incumbent, faces Republican Kevin Lincoln, a former mayor of Stockton. Prop 50's redistricting moved Lincoln out of his previous district; he opted to run here.

CA-22
Toss-up
Central Valley · Bakersfield, Visalia, Hanford

Republican incumbent David Valadao, a dairy farmer first elected in 2012, holds a seat where registered Democrats outnumber Republicans by double digits. He voted to impeach Trump in 2021. Democrat Randy Villegas, a Visalia school board trustee and College of the Sequoias political science professor, won the June primary with 32.4% to Valadao's 40.7%.

CA-27
Lean Dem
Northern LA County · Antelope Valley, Santa Clarita

Democratic incumbent George Whitesides, a former NASA Chief of Staff and former CEO of Virgin Galactic, flipped this seat in 2024 with 51.3%. He faces Republican Jason Gibbs, a city councilmember and engineer. Prop 50's redistricting removed some conservative suburbs, shifting the district's partisan composition. Primary results were close: Gibbs 41.0% · Whitesides 40.6%.

CA-41
Lean Dem
LA / Orange County · Cerritos, Lakewood, La Mirada area

Prop 50 renumbered this district. Democratic incumbent Linda Sánchez, who has served in Congress since 2003 (previously representing CA-38), is now running here. She faces Republican Mitchell Clemmons. Longtime CA-41 Republican Ken Calvert moved to the newly drawn CA-40 under the new map.

CA-11
Dem vs. Dem
San Francisco · open seat

Nancy Pelosi's seat is open for the first time since 1987. Under California's top-two primary, two Democrats advanced to the general election: State Senator Scott Wiener and San Francisco Supervisor Connie Chan. Wiener is generally described as the moderate; Chan as the progressive. The two have taken opposing positions on housing policy. Pelosi has endorsed Chan.
